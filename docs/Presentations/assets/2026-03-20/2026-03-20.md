**Introduction**
Title: 3DGS & alpamayo & RL Fin Tuning

A seminar covering 3DGS-based simulation, NVIDIA's Alpamayo ecosystem from GTC 2026, GenAD quantization for edge deployment, and GRPO reinforcement learning for diffusion planners.

**Section 1: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)**

3DGS represents scenes using millions of explicit 3D Gaussians, achieving real-time rendering (100+ FPS) — a massive leap over NeRF (~1 FPS). For autonomous driving, this enables photorealistic closed-loop simulation built directly from camera footage, with no sim-to-real gap and no manual 3D modeling. HUGSIM is the current best open-source 3DGS simulator, supporting nuScenes, Waymo, KITTI-360, and PandaSet. A key lab application: reconstructing our C-Track into a photorealistic simulator for RL training.

**Section 2: GTC 2026 — Alpamayo & AlpaSim**
GTC 2026 highlighted the widening gap between academia and industry. NVIDIA's Alpamayo 1.5 upgrades to Cosmos-Reason2, adds RL post-training (+45% reasoning quality), text-guided planning, flexible camera configs, and VQA. AlpaSim provides an open-source closed-loop simulation platform powered by neural rendering, supporting multiple E2E policies (Alpamayo-R1, VaVAM, Transfuser). Two practical use cases: using the full 10B-param Alpamayo as an offline teacher for dataset generation via distillation, and deploying distilled models on DRIVE AGX Thor with TensorRT Edge-LLM.

**Section 3: GenAD Quantization**
Quantizing GenAD by separating Head and Backbone independently, following Autoware's approach where perception and planning heads have different quantization tolerances. Backbone separation is completed. Using NVIDIA's DL4AGX toolkit for systematic TensorRT conversion. Goal: contribute our GenAD quantization pipeline to the DL4AGX repository.

**Section 4: GRPO Diffusion-Planner**
Diffusion-Planner is a Transformer-based diffusion model for E2E trajectory planning (ICLR 2025 Oral), evaluated on nuPlan with closed-loop simulation. Overall performance already exceeds baseline benchmarks, but the remaining challenge is improving Test-14 — a subset of difficult scenarios. An initial attempt to feed velocity into the Transformer failed, leading to the analysis that the issue stems from insufficient training data, not architectural limitations. This pointed to reinforcement learning as the solution.


Approach 1 — Open-Loop GRPO: Applied standard RL with reward shaping on nuPlan data across 6 experiments. Reward redesign improved scores from 0.65 to 0.80, but increasing comfort weight caused policy collapse, and a KL penalty bug was discovered. Even after fixes, evaluation degraded by -75% to -80%. Conclusion: GRPO is fundamentally incompatible with diffusion models — score matching loss does not yield valid log probabilities, and iterative denoising amplifies small weight changes into compounding errors.

Approach 2 — Closed-Loop RL via Waymax: Since nuPlan lacks GPU parallelization for RL-scale training, we turned to Waymax (by Waymo/Google), which offers full GPU parallelization via JAX, closed-loop reactive simulation, and the Waymo Open Dataset. V-Max bridges Waymax and nuPlan, enabling training with closed-loop RL on Waymax and evaluation on nuPlan benchmarks. This is a significant research gap — very few have attempted closed-loop RL for diffusion planners, and even fewer have cross-evaluated Waymax-trained models on nuPlan.


**Section 5: Autoware & FOV Test**
Autoware Reporting: Full vehicle deployment via Autoware was out of scope, so we narrowed to only the packages required for quantized E2E model (B2D-family) deployment: Autoware_TensorRT_GenAD, Autoware_HLC_Publisher, Autoware_Trajectory_Selector, and Autoware_Evaluation_Tools. Goal: contribute these packages to Autoware via Pull Request.


FOV Test: Investigated how BEV features and model performance change when matching the RAVEE vehicle's FOV in the Bench2Drive environment. Counterintuitively, narrower FOV (60°) outperformed wider FOV (70°) — driving score improved by +10.4%, layout collisions dropped by -61%, and stop line violations decreased by -35%. The reason: the effective training FOV after crop is approximately 60°, so matching it yields higher resolution on the critical forward region and less edge distortion for cleaner BEV transformation.
