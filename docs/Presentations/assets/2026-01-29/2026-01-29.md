### **Introduction**

Title: **Why Diffusion Models are the Future of E2E Autonomous Driving Planning**

Introduction In the rapidly evolving landscape of End-to-End (E2E) autonomous driving, the shift from simple regression-based planning to generative modeling is opening new doors. My recent research focuses on leveraging Diffusion Models to overcome the limitations of conventional planning and building a robust MLOps infrastructure to support these complex experiments.

Section 1: The Shift to Diffusion-Based Planning
Why move toward Diffusion? Traditional Autoregressive (Regression-based) models often suffer from "mean prediction" issues—averaging out possible paths in multimodal distributions—and accumulate errors over time.

Diffusion Models offer a superior alternative by:

Refining the entire trajectory simultaneously through iterative denoising.

Enabling multimodal planning, allowing the vehicle to consider multiple diverse and valid paths.

Supporting RL Fine-tuning, which makes it easier to directly optimize planning quality compared to models like VAD or UniAD.

Section 2: Two Pillars of DiffusionDrive—U-Net vs. Transformer
My analysis identifies two primary architectural axes in this field:

U-Net-based (Spatial Refinement): Represented by DiffusionDrive, this approach uses CNN-based encoder-decoders to refine predefined anchor trajectories. It is computationally efficient, requires less data, and excels at spatial path generation in BEV space.

Transformer-based (Context-Aware Reasoning): Represented by Diffusion-Planner, this models trajectories as sequences of tokens. While computationally heavier, it excels at capturing spatiotemporal relationships and causal reasoning.
